# Monopoly lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **A possible secret.** The pitch is that "physical stores can't A/B test" and that the cheap step is a simulation before the floor test. The leader the founder names, RetailNext, measures what already happened and runs physical pilot-vs-control tests. StoreLab adds the step before that: AI-designed changes, checked against constraints and simulated first. If simulation really cuts failed physical pilots, that is a new capability, not a small improvement.
- **The data could compound.** The founder wants real pilot results fed back to recalibrate the simulator, and the long-term metric is "does it rank real winners above real losers?" Every pilot adds evidence no copycat has. That is the one durable advantage this lens can see: proprietary know-how that grows with use.
- **Cheaper to install than the leader.** RetailNext's analytics run on its own sensor. StoreLab is hardware-agnostic and uses the store's existing CCTV and POS exports. That lowers the cost of buying it and makes rollout across a whole chain easier.
- **Clear trust rules.** Deterministic numbers, hard limits that overrule the AI, anonymous tracking only. These make it easier for a cautious buyer to say yes.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **It is not yet 10x better at anything, and it may never be.** The only evidence is synthetic data made by a model from the same family as the simulator. The founder says this himself. If real shoppers do not follow the fitted model, the "breakthrough" becomes a heatmap tool with an AI front end. That market is already crowded, and RetailNext leads it with 600+ retailers.
2. **An incumbent takes the step.** RetailNext already has paths, POS data, pilot testing and offices in Tokyo, Shanghai and Singapore. Adding a simulator plus a Gemini-style agent on top of its data is an easy extension for them. Nothing in the brief stops a copycat in month six except real calibration data StoreLab does not have yet.
3. **No plan for distribution.** The brief has no team size, no budget, no sales channel and no first customer. Enterprise retail sales cycles and the cost of each pilot are unknown. A product with no way to reach buyers is not a business.

- Which single chain will give you real CCTV and POS data within 60 days, and why that chain?
- How many real pilots does a retailer need to see before they trust the ranking? (Your own open question. It decides how long it takes to build the advantage.)
- Who else sells AI layout simulation to Philippine or JAPAC chains today? Needs /founder-competitors.
- What does the first customer cost to win, and what will they pay per store or per pilot? Needs /founder-pricing and /founder-cfo.
- Is the CCTV mid-size chains already have good enough for the path pipeline? Test this on real footage, not the demo clip.
- Why now? Is it cheap vision models, cheap cloud, or something else you know?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Narrow down first.** Commit to one segment and one category of decision (for example, endcap and display placement) before going wider.
2. **Get real data.** Sign a single design-partner chain that will share real data and run at least one simulated-then-physical pilot with test and control stores.
3. **Report the real ranking result.** Show whether the simulator's ranking matched the real-world result, even if it is weak. This is the start of the moat.
4. **Write down the path to the first 10 chains.** Show the channel and the cost per customer (/founder-consumer, /founder-cfo).

## Recommended first customer segment
**(a) Mid-size Philippine convenience and mini-mart chains (20–500 stores, existing CCTV).** This lens wants a small market that can be dominated first, and (a) is the one that is small, nameable and winnable.
- **Small enough to own.** It is one country and one format, with a short list of chains. Winning a large share of it is possible. "JAPAC grocery" (b) is a large, crowded fight against an incumbent with offices across the region.
- **Fits the product.** Many near-identical stores make clean test-vs-control pilots, and the MVP is already modelled on a convenience store. Filipino and English goals are supported.
- **Where the leader's model is weakest.** Using existing CCTV avoids the cost of proprietary sensors. Whether that matters to these buyers is an open question for /founder-consumer.
- **Speeds up the moat.** Repeated formats mean each pilot generalises across stores, so calibration data builds up faster.
- (c) Mall specialty stores have unique layouts and fewer stores alike, so pilots are harder and each lesson is harder to reuse.

Expand later into (b) once the ranking record exists. Price is still open (/founder-pricing).
