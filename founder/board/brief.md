# Board brief: StoreLab

## The idea (from founder/idea.md)

- **What it is:** A/B testing for physical retail. Software that turns existing store CCTV and POS data into a behavioural "digital twin" of a store. A store manager states a goal in plain language (e.g. "increase snack sales 10%, budget ₱30k, don't worsen the checkout queue"); an AI agent (Google Gemini) designs concrete store changes (move a display, add an endcap, swap aisles); each change is checked against physical/business constraints and simulated on synthetic shoppers fitted to the store's real behaviour; the best one is turned into a real-store pilot plan (test vs control stores, duration from a power calculation, success metric).
- **Who it is for:** physical retailers that change layouts and displays without knowing what will work. **First segment is undecided — the founder wants the board to recommend one of:** (a) mid-size Philippine convenience/mini-mart chains, 20–500 stores, with existing CCTV; (b) JAPAC supermarket/grocery chains; (c) mall-based specialty/department-store retailers.
- **What it sells, at what price:** a SaaS platform (store analytics + AI experiment design + simulation + pilot planning). **Price not decided** (per-store subscription and per-pilot fees are both being considered).
- **Where and how:** software-first and hardware-agnostic (uses cameras the store already has plus POS exports); runs on Google Cloud; JAPAC-first (multilingual goals in English, Japanese, Korean, Bahasa, Filipino); anonymous tracking only — no faces, no identity, no cross-visit tracking.
- **Stage and constraints:** a hackathon entry (AI Builder Cup by Hack2skill, Retail & Commerce theme), submission due 2026-10-18. A working MVP exists but only on synthetic demo data for one fictional convenience store. No customers, no pilots, no real store data. Funding budget and team size were not stated.

## What the founder has told us beyond the idea

- **The pitch:** "E-commerce can A/B test almost everything. Physical stores can't. StoreLab lets retailers test promising store changes virtually before changing the real floor." Today's cycle is "idea → physically change store → wait → measure → undo"; StoreLab aims for "observe → generate ideas → simulate → shortlist → physically test → learn".
- **Known competitor:** RetailNext — an established in-store analytics company (traffic counting, shopper paths, heatmaps, POS correlation, physical pilot-vs-control testing) that lists offices in Tokyo, Shanghai and Singapore and says it serves 600+ retailers in 100+ countries. Its analytics products are built around its own proprietary sensor. The founder positions StoreLab as extending measurement into AI-designed, pre-simulated experiments, not as a cheaper RetailNext, and explicitly does not claim the observation layer (heatmaps, paths, POS correlation) is new.
- **What the MVP demonstrates (synthetic data, clearly labelled):** a camera pipeline that recovers anonymous shopper paths (about 95% of shoppers found in a demo clip); a behaviour model fitted from 10,000 synthetic journeys that reproduces the store's observed numbers within about 12%; on test experiments, the simulator ranks options in the same order as the hidden "true" world that generated the data. Because the data is synthetic and generated by a model of the same family, this proves the mechanism works, not that it predicts real shoppers.
- **Key product principles the founder holds:** the AI never computes the numbers (a deterministic simulator does); hard constraints (budget, refrigeration, fire exits, aisle width, checkout congestion limits) override the AI; predictions are hypotheses for real-world testing, not guarantees; real pilot results should feed back to recalibrate the simulator.
- **The long-term metric the founder cares about:** "does the simulator rank real-world winners above real-world losers?"
- **Open questions the founder already knows about:** pricing, first customer segment, whether retailers' existing CCTV is good enough, and how many real pilots are needed before a retailer trusts the simulation.
